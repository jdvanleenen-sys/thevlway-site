"What am I paying for?" takes four answers. What it costs a month. What that comes to in a year. Whether renting it makes sense. And what you use it for.

One business owner asked that question five months and $445 too late.

Her AI could have helped the whole time.

## One owner paid $445 for one button

She's an electrician. She has four invoices to send this week, and her spreadsheet is open.

"Invoices again. I need software."

Then the ads pop up. ([0:13 in the video](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhiVFpwijoA&t=13s>)) It's one made-up invoice app with three plans.

- **Free plan:** 3 invoices a month
- **$29 a month:** unlimited invoices
- **$89 a month:** EVERYTHING!

Four invoices in one week is more than the free plan's three a month. So she does need a paid plan.

The $29 plan would do the job. The $89 plan has the confetti.

"Everything! Perfect."

She clicks $89.

## Five months later, she uses one button

The months go by. ([0:27](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhiVFpwijoA&t=27s>))

$89. $178. $267. $356. $445.

And what does she use?

One button. Send.

The other buttons gather dust. Payroll. Fleet. Inventory. Reports.

## Renting the software was never the mistake

This is the part that's easy to get backwards.

Sending invoices is a basic business task. Renting software is fine.

She didn't go wrong by renting. She went wrong by renting more than the job needs.

She still rents at the end of this story.

If you're deciding what to rent and what to build yourself, [build vs buy software](/blog/build-vs-buy-software/) runs two questions on that.

## What am I paying for? The 4-part check

Her AI could have told her on day one. She just had to put her statement in front of it and ask.

So finally, she does. ([0:42](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhiVFpwijoA&t=42s>))

"What am I paying for?"

Here's what the AI does, in order.

### 1. Name the charge and the month

"Invoice app: $89 a month."

Just the plain line off the statement.

### 2. Work out the year

"That's $1,068 a year." ([0:49](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhiVFpwijoA&t=49s>))

$89 a month doesn't sound like much. $89 times 12 is a different conversation.

### 3. Ask if renting makes sense

"Sending invoices is a basic business task. Renting software is fine."

So the app stays. The question now is how much of it.

### 4. Ask what you use it for

"What do you use it for?" ([1:01](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhiVFpwijoA&t=61s>))

This is the question only she can answer. An AI can read a statement. It can't know what she clicks all day.

"One button. Send."

## The decision stays with you

Look at what the AI says next.

"Your call."

It never tells her to cancel. It never tells her to switch. It lays out the numbers and asks the question. The decision is hers.

She makes it herself. ([1:10](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhiVFpwijoA&t=70s>))

"The $29 plan sends invoices. Switching."

She still rents. She just rents what the job needs.

## Don't let a chat do your adding

If you run this check with AI, watch the math.

An AI chat isn't a calculator. It can slip a digit on a long column and still sound sure.

So when I built this check, I had the AI read the statement and a small script do the adding. If you're working in a plain chat, add up the total yourself.

And look at what's on the page before you share it. The statement in the video shows one line and no card number. Keep yours the same way.

## Rent what the job needs

Here's the rule from the video. ([1:15](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhiVFpwijoA&t=75s>))

Rent what the job needs, with your eyes open.

## Your move today

What's one tool you pay for every month and have never really questioned?

Write down three things for it.

1. What it costs a month.
2. What that comes to in a year.
3. What you use it for.

Then it's your call.
