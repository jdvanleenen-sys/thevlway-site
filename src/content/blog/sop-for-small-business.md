An SOP for a small business is a written way of doing a job, so that someone else can do it the same way. The version an AI can run is 1 folder with 4 plain lines in it. You pick the folder, you write the 4 lines, and you hand it to a stranger to see if it works.

That sounds like a lot. It's 3 steps, and no code.

## SOP for small business: why your AI guesses

You made your 6 folders. Then you asked your AI for a quote. It guessed the price, and it guessed what to send.

So you fixed it by hand, the way you always have. And the quote is still your job.

You'd think the fix is a smarter AI. It isn't. The difference is structure.

A list of 6 folders is a start. A map is when your AI can read it and act. Each folder says what goes in it, what the AI does, what comes out, and what you check before it goes anywhere.

If you don't have your 6 folders yet, start with [small business systems](/blog/small-business-systems/). This post picks up right after that.

## Step 1: pick 1 folder

This step gives you 1 real folder to build, not 6 at once.

You don't train a new hire on every job in 1 morning.

So ask, "Which stage of my work leaks the most right now?" The stages are Leads, Quotes, Jobs and Payments. Pick the 1 that leaks.

Take Andre. He's made up, but his week will sound familiar. He runs a small cleaning company, and his quotes go out days late. He picks Quotes.

Here's why. You want a real working folder, not a practice one.

The mistake is starting all 6 at once. The right move is 1 folder, finished before you start the next.

That's your folder. Keep it for step 2. ([Step 1 in the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YOUTUBE_ID&t=75s))

## Step 2: write its 4 parts

This step tells your AI what to do inside that folder.

Think of a work order. It says what's coming in, what to do, what to hand back, and who signs off.

So take your folder, and write 4 lines in it:

- **What goes in**
- **What my AI does with it**
- **What comes out**
- **What I check before it goes anywhere**

For Andre's Quotes folder, what goes in is his walkthrough notes. His AI drafts the quote from his price list. What comes out is a draft quote. And Andre checks the price before it goes anywhere.

Here's why this works. Your AI doesn't know your business. These 4 lines tell it.

The mistake is a folder with a name and nothing inside. The right move is 4 plain lines. You don't need code.

If you want to practice first, there's a free hands-on folder builder in the lesson. In the builder, those 4 parts are called input, guidance, output and check. The folder method is from Jake Van Clief and David McDermott.

That's 1 folder with 4 parts. Hold onto it for step 3. ([Step 2 in the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YOUTUBE_ID&t=111s))

## Step 3: hand it to a stranger

This step shows whether your folder works without you in the room.

Think of a new hire on day 1. If they call you 5 times, the work order had gaps.

So give your folder to someone who doesn't know your business. A family member works, or another member of the community. Tell them, "Use this folder with your AI, and don't ask me anything."

Back to Andre. He hands his Quotes folder to his brother. His brother has never quoted a cleaning job. His quote comes out close to Andre's. So the folder passed its first test.

If they get lost, you just found the exact gap to fix.

The mistake is testing it yourself. You fill the gaps from memory. The right move is a stranger.

It was never the AI. It was the structure you built. If you're still sorting out where the structure goes, the Government of Canada keeps a [small business hub](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-small-business/en) with guides on planning and running a business. ([Step 3 in the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YOUTUBE_ID&t=174s))

## Why the order matters

Pick 1 folder. Write its 4 parts. Hand it to a stranger.

Skip step 1, and you may end up with 6 half-built folders. Skip step 2, and your AI is back to guessing. Skip step 3, and you may not see where the gaps are.
